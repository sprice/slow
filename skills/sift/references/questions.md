# Questions each field asks

A prompt list for choosing lenses. Each line is a question a specialist would put to the case, with an idea it often leads to. Use it to notice what you have not asked. It is not a checklist, and most cases need ideas that are not here. A question that turns up nothing in the facts gets dropped.

## When people are in conflict

- **Who was blamed, and does the blame match their share of the cause?** Scapegoating. Check whether it happened, and whether it happened more than once, since the first accusers can become the next target.
- **What were people copying from each other?** Imitation of wants, outrage, and enemies; cascades.
- **How is a grievance supposed to be handled here?** Settled privately, fought over, or taken to an audience. Moral cultures differ on this.
- **What could not be traded off?** Sacred values, where proposing the trade is the offence.
- **What did the group reward?** Status from purity, from suffering, or from being first to condemn.
- **What went unsaid?** Hidden views that make a room look unanimous.
- **Who left quietly and who stayed to argue?** Exit and voice.
- **How does the critics' own framework describe it?** State it in its terms before weighing it.

## Money, rules, and institutions

- **Who paid, who benefited, and did the sums work?** Fixed costs, market size, incentives.
- **What did the rules allow or require?** Law, contracts, liability, regulation.
- **Whose interests differed from the people they acted for?** Principal and agent problems.
- **What happens when everyone does the sensible thing for themselves?** Collective action problems; Olson; Ostrom.

## Time

- **Has this happened before, and how did it end then?** Precedent. For a base rate, ask how often the outcome occurs across a relevant set of cases.
- **What earlier decision made this one likely?** Path dependence.
- **What changed just before?** Technology, law, demographics, a single event.

## The natural world

- **What is conserved, and what limits apply?** Conservation laws, the speed of light, thermodynamics.
- **What is the mechanism, step by step?** If a step cannot be stated, the explanation is incomplete.
- **At what scale does the explanation hold?** An answer right for a room can be wrong for a galaxy.
- **Who first posed the problem, and what did earlier answers get wrong?** The history of a question often teaches the idea best.

## Belief, meaning, and texts

- **What do the sources say, and when were they written?**
- **What premises does each tradition start from, and what follows if they are granted?**
- **What question was this teaching answering in its own time?**
- **What changed in how people lived that made the question feel different?**

## Every case

- **Does the thing being explained happen, and how often?**
- **What would be true if the leading explanation were wrong?**
- **Which explanation did nobody in the story have a reason to offer?**
