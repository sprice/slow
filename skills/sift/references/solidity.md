# Solidity

Every lens and every claim in the account sits on one of four kinds of ground. The label tells the reader how hard they can lean on it. The wording has to match the label, because a reader hears confidence in the phrasing before they read the tag.

Label the idea by its standing among people who study it. Say separately how well the facts of this case fit it. A well-established idea can still fit a particular case poorly.

## Established

Repeatedly confirmed, and no serious specialist disputes it. Conservation of energy. Natural selection. That people conform under group pressure in laboratory settings.

Wording: plain present tense. "Light takes time to travel." No hedge.

## Well supported

Strong evidence from several independent directions, with live argument about scope, size, or mechanism. Most good social science sits here at best.

Wording: confident, with the limit stated. "Studies across many countries find this, though the size of the effect varies."

## Debated

Serious scholars hold it and serious scholars reject it. Often the observed behaviour is solid while the theory built on it is not. Say which part is which. That scapegoating happens is established. That it is the origin of culture is debated.

Wording: attribute it. "Girard argued that", "on this view", "critics reply that". Do not use "because" for a debated claim.

## Speculative

A plausible idea with thin evidence, or one that fits this case only by analogy. Worth including when it is the best available explanation for a part nothing else explains.

Wording: mark it openly. "One possibility is", "this would explain X, but nothing here confirms it".

## Fields without experiments

Theology, philosophy, and much of history do not settle questions by measurement. Use the same labels for what they can support. "Established" means the sources or the text plainly say it, or the argument is accepted as valid across traditions. "Debated" means traditions or schools divide on it. Do not rank a tradition's premises as true or false. Say what follows if they are granted.

## Check before sending

- Does each lens point to a fact in this case?
- Does any sentence sound more certain than its label allows?
- Is every "because" attached to something established or well supported?
- If nothing in the reply is marked debated or unknown, is that true of the subject or an omission?
